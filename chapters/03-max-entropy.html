4.1 Energy-based cooperative games

A striking application of the maximum-entropy principle is to valuation problems in machine learning: how much does a feature, data point, or player contribute to an outcome? The classical answer is the Shapley value from cooperative game theory, but it is one particular choice among many.

In Energy-Based Learning for Cooperative Games (Bian et al. 2022), the valuation problem is recast through an energy / maximum-entropy lens. A cooperative game with characteristic function \(v(S)\) over coalitions \(S \subseteq N\) induces a Gibbs distribution over coalitions,

\[ p(S) \propto \exp\big(v(S) / \tau\big), \]

where \(\tau\) controls the temperature. Applying the maximum-entropy principle to game-theoretic valuations yields a new class of valuation methods, of which the Shapley value arises as a special case.

4.2 Why this is useful

  • Principled interpretation. Valuations become the expectation of a well-defined quantity under a maximum-entropy distribution, giving a principled framework for interpreting AI models.
  • A family, not a point. The temperature \(\tau\) traces a spectrum of valuation schemes, enabling trade-offs beyond the single Shapley solution.
  • Bridges to physics. The same Gibbs machinery links directly to the statistical-physics origins of the Boltzmann machine.

4.3 Takeaway

Maximizing entropy turns “which valuation should I use?” into “which constraints do I believe?” — a shift that clarifies assumptions and unifies existing methods under one roof.

Bian, Yatao, Yu Rong, Tingyang Xu, Jiaxiang Wu, Andreas Krause, and Junzhou Huang. 2022. “Energy-Based Learning for Cooperative Games, with Applications to Valuation Problems in Machine Learning.” International Conference on Learning Representations (ICLR). https://openreview.net/forum?id=xLfAgCroImw.
